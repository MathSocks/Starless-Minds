### Script

A private research paper not available to the public and only accessible through Lexafell. There is a notice at the beginning of the paper noting the illegal possession, replication or sharing of this document can lead to fines or academic excommunication.

**This particular paper requires Lexafell Associate Professor clearance.**

Details the study and subsequent dissection of ten Loksarian soldiers (8 men and 2 women). Under what circumstances these soldiers were acquired is not specified.

5 of the 8 men were Tieflings, 1 goliath, 1 Yuan-ti and 1 human.
The two females were Tieflings.

**The report goes over the full body dissection before focussing in on the brain dissections.**

The two female soldiers both had their wombs surgically removed. One of the two had a double mastectomy.
Individuals ranged from ages 19 to 40. (In some cases exact age could not be verified due to lack of legal documentation.)

<u> General dissection notes </u>

All suffered some level of damage to the cilia within the cochlea to varying degrees.
All suffered a variety of physical wounds, however, of the small sample group none died of the wounds.

- 5 of the 10 soldiers died of dysentery.
- 4 of the 10 soldiers died of gangrene or other forms of wound infection.
- 1 of the soldiers died of blood loss.
- 9 of the 10 soldiers suffered from trench foot varying from moderate to severe.
- The 10th soldier suffered from minor trench foot.

All soldiers displayed certain differences from the average South Edranite in the gut microbiome. Key differences were a lower microbial diversity, in particular reduced levels of actinobacteria, lentisphaeria, and verrucomicrobia.

<u> Brain dissection </u>

7 of the 10 soldiers suffered some from Chronic Traumatic Encephalopathy (CTE). Gilthana suspects that a large portion of the warfare force suffers cognitive dysregulation including memory retention, mood regulation, dementia and likely extended Post concussion syndrome (PCS).

All of the soldiers displayed a reduced Hippocampus, damaged/hyperactive amygdala, and impaired connectivity of the amygdala-ventromedial prefrontal cortex. Giltnana suggests this impaired connectivity is due to heightened stress of their mother during incubation.
