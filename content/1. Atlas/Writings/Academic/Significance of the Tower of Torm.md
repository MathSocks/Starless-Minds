### Script

_Lexafell Academy publishes the paper_ "The significance of the Tower of Torm" _with the intent of exploring the cultural importance of the Tower of Torm, specifically as a spiritual and political landmark._
_This paper is owned by Lexafell Academy, and is accessible to the public audience of South Edren._

---

**Subject:**
The comprehensive history of the Tower of Torm, and Starfell Lake.

**A Tower in the Eye of God:**
Prior to the existence/construction of the Tower of Torm, the residents of the land worshiped a lake they had titled the "Eye of God".
Imagery depicting this God showed a floating head with an eye in its centre.

Researchers later theorised that these people considered the Beholder species as a physical avatar of the lake, and thus treated it as a sacred creature. Most of the artefacts supporting this suspicion are preserved archaeological items found around Starfell lake.

After the Tower's appearance, it is thought that the holy structure was correlated into the "Eye of the God" belief system, before becoming the more preferred religion of the two.

In present-day Torm, little of the once major reverence the lake held remains amongst the Tormians.

**Mythological Inception:**
Tormtar, the belief system that worships the God Torm, has multiple origin stories. One tale is that Torm was called forth by 20 men who were unjustly sentenced to death. They wept into Starfell lake, and Torm arose from its waters, becoming a new pillar of justice.
Though there are other narratives known amongst the adherents of Tormtar, this one is the most common recollection.

Naturally though, scholars have their own conclusions of how Torm and Torm Tower came to be.

Following the events of the 20 men, Torm emerged from the lake, and proceeds to either climb or build the Tower of Torm, depending on the source. This is where TIR informal nickname: "The King who Climbs" stems from.\
Some believe that Torm also shed TIR skin as an act of cleansing from mortal sin.

Another well-known story is that Torm was summoned by the collective sorrows of an unfair King's people. This one is popular to use as a form of slander against an unpopular figurehead.

**Political Significance:**
The Tower of Torm is a considerable and major part of the political culture of South Edren.

An example of this is how prominent authorities and political figures are greatly encouraged to visit the tower, as a sign of faith and commitment to their people. Failure or refusal to do so can be interpreted negatively, and heavily damage the reputation and image of the individual.
Additionally, the refusal to anoint one's head from a Tormtar cleric is often viewed as a sign the individual is corrupt and self serving. In some cases, it is also seen as a sign they are destined for death.

**Tormtar:**
Tormtar is the religious order of Torm. TIR followers greatly inhabit the city surrounding Torm Tower, as well as within the sacred structure itself. TIR adherents are said to and follow TIR will and teachings.

The Tower is commonly visited by the public for blessings and/or guidance, prior to pursuing a difficult task or performing a personal sacrifice. It is believed that visiting the Tower during the day brings good luck, whereas entering the structure at night was an act that displeased Torm, and as such would result in your disappearance. Thus the popular explanation of how the disappearances of Torm Tower occur.

The academic explanation is that inside the Tower, rays of radiance emit and destroy living matter within its walls. These rays are also thought to be the origin for the condition Torm Blues.
